# Escento Copy Style

This is the canonical guide for user-facing text in Escento. It applies to pages, forms, metadata, errors, empty states, admin tools, accessibility text, and transactional email.

## Voice

- Direct, concise, and plainspoken.
- Specific about what the user can do.
- Confident without sounding grand or promotional.
- Mildly conversational. Use contractions when they sound natural.
- Short sentences are welcome.

Write `Find musicians who fit the project.` instead of `Discover talented musicians and unlock meaningful creative opportunities.`

## Avoid

- Never use an em dash (`—`) in user-facing copy.
- Avoid startup filler: discover, unlock, empower, elevate, seamless, journey, vibrant, curated, amazing, meaningful, perfect, ecosystem, and world-class.
- Avoid abstract slogans and fake profundity.
- Do not force music metaphors into routine UI. Use stage, spotlight, soundcheck, rhythm, room, and similar language only when the literal term would be worse.
- Avoid repetitive three-part slogans, empty adjectives, excessive reassurance, and explanations of obvious controls.

## Marketing copy

State what Escento does: musicians create profiles, creators post gigs, and either side can send a request. Personality is useful only when the sentence stays concrete. One clear sentence beats a polished paragraph.

Good: `Post what you need. Let musicians decide if it fits.`

Bad: `Bring your creative vision to life through meaningful collaboration.`

## Buttons and links

Name the action in 1 to 4 words: `Browse musicians`, `Post a gig`, `Send request`, `Save changes`, `Delete account`.

Avoid vague labels such as `Get started`, `Explore`, `Learn more`, or `Take the next step` when a specific action exists.

## Forms

- Keep labels literal and short.
- Use helper text only when it prevents a mistake or explains a consequence.
- Do not put essential instructions only in placeholders.
- Use sentence case for labels and buttons.
- Match validation text to the actual rule.

Good: `Add the instruments you actively play.`

Bad: `Tell us about the instruments that represent your unique musical background.`

## Errors and success messages

Say what happened and what to do next. Keep it to 1 or 2 sentences. Do not blame the user or expose internal service errors.

Good: `We couldn't save your profile. Try again.`

Bad: `Something went wrong while processing your request. Please try again later.`

## Empty states

State the empty condition, then offer one useful action when available.

Good: `No messages yet.` and `Browse musicians`

Bad: `Your inbox is quiet. Start making meaningful connections today!`

## Metadata

- Titles should name the page and Escento.
- Descriptions should be one factual sentence, ideally under 155 characters.
- Avoid keyword lists and claims the product cannot prove.

Example title: `Escento | Find Musicians and Gigs`

Example description: `Find musicians, post gigs, and message people directly on Escento.`

## FAQ

Use questions a real user would ask. Answer in the first sentence, stay short, and do not repeat the question. Describe only shipped behavior.

Good: `No. Escento does not handle payments. Arrange payment directly with the other person.`

## Accessibility text

Keep labels and alt text literal. Describe the control or meaningful image, not the mood. Decorative images should use empty alt text.

## Legal copy

Do not casually rewrite Terms, Privacy, Compliance, consent language, or destructive warnings. Ordinary headings and surrounding UI may be clarified, but substantive changes require legal review.
